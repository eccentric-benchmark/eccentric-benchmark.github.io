Method

How a logical error rate is made

ECCentric takes a code's syndrome-extraction circuit, compiles it onto a device, adds the device's noise, samples it with Stim and decodes it. Every result in the benchmark is one path through these stages, and every stage is a set of interchangeable modules, so codes, devices, noise models and decoders can be compared on equal terms.

Codes

The benchmark covers 70 codes with results, from the surface code to the bivariate bicycle codes IBM plans to build, grouped by the principle they are built on. Each code comes with its syndrome-extraction schedule; some have several, compared in the benchmark. The Codes page draws each code's circuit and lists its results.

Operations

Most results are memory experiments: prepare the logical qubits, run the syndrome circuit for as many rounds as the code distance, and measure, in the Z or the X basis. On top of memory, the benchmark runs

  • lattice surgery on rotated surface-code patches, built with tqec: a patch's memory, a merge (ZZ or XX measurement), and the lattice-surgery CNOT;
  • fault-tolerant operations on CSS codes: logical Z and X measurements, joint ZZ and XX measurements, and a transversal CNOT between two blocks, where decoders see the errors that cross the gate.

An operation fails when any of its logical observables comes out wrong.

Devices

Codes run on 25 device models: IBM's Heron, Flamingo, Loon/Nighthawk and the bicycle architecture module (with projected multi-QPU chains for codes too large for any announced device), Google Willow, Quantinuum Apollo, Infleqtion and the Harvard/MIT neutral-atom array, each with and without shuttling where the hardware moves qubits, and synthetic topologies from a line to all-to-all. A native layout, built for one code with a coupler for every pair of qubits its circuit entangles, shows what the code achieves without SWAPs. The Devices page draws each device's couplers and lists its noise.

Compilation

The code's circuit is compiled onto the device with Qiskit: a layout places the circuit's qubits on the device's (trivial, dense or SABRE), and routing inserts SWAPs wherever a two-qubit gate acts on uncoupled qubits (basic, SABRE, stochastic or lookahead). The gates are then translated to the device's native gate set with Qiskit, BQSKit or TKET. Gates on couplers between QPUs, or on long-range couplers marked as noisy, get the remote error and duration. Routing is where many codes lose most: see routing overhead, translation overhead and routing against native layouts.

Noise

Noise is added gate by gate to the compiled circuit. The benchmark uses 27 noise models:

  • circuit-level models with one parameter p: SD6 (uniform depolarizing) and SI1000 (superconducting-inspired), among others;
  • hardware models of IBM Heron and Flamingo, Google Willow (from Google's paper and from its spec sheet), Quantinuum Apollo, Infleqtion and the Harvard/MIT array, with one- and two-qubit gate errors, readout errors, leakage, shuttling errors and atom loss as each platform reports them;
  • scaled hardware models: every error probability of a hardware model multiplied by 0.1, 0.25 or 0.5, to see how far each device is from protecting a code.

On devices with T1 and T2 (IBM, Infleqtion, Harvard/MIT), every qubit decays for as long as it waits in each layer of the circuit, for example while other qubits are measured (1.56 µs readout on IBM devices, as on Heron today). Crosstalk is not modelled. Atom loss is modelled per qubit, and where the hardware detects it, the decoder is told where losses happened.

Decoding

The noisy circuit is sampled with Stim, and its detector error model is handed to every decoder that applies to the code: 14 decoders, from matching to belief propagation, GARI and the Tesseract search decoder. Each decoder sees the same shots, so differences between decoders are not sampling noise. The Decoders page compares them.

How results are measured and reported

A logical error rate is the fraction of shots in which any logical qubit failed over all syndrome-extraction rounds, with a 95% Wilson interval. The per-round rate assumes independent rounds: ε = (1 − (1 − 2P)1/rounds)/2. Each configuration is sampled until 100 logical errors are seen or its job's shot or time budget runs out; results short of 100 errors are refined with more shots in the background. Every result carries a status:

  • measured: at least 100 logical errors seen;
  • low statistics: stopped by the shot or time limit before 100 errors;
  • upper bound: no logical error seen; the 95% upper bound is reported;
  • does not fit: the code needs more qubits than the device has;
  • decoder not applicable: the decoder refused the circuit, e.g. matching on errors that do not decompose into edges;
  • timeout: the decoder did not finish a single batch of shots within the time limit.

A compiled circuit can have a lower distance than its code: a SWAP between two data qubits, added by routing, can let a single fault flip a logical qubit unnoticed. The Explorer marks such circuits. The dataset's columns are documented in docs/dataset.md.

Versions

The version evaluated in the SIGMETRICS'26 paper is tagged v1.0.0-sigmetrics26. The benchmark has kept growing since: more codes, devices, decoders and shots, and refined device models, so its numbers can differ from the paper's (changelog). The Findings are always drawn from the current version; the results as published are in the paper.
