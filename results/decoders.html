Picking a decoder before decoding. QEC Matchmaker, trained on this dataset, predicts from a circuit alone, in seconds, whether it is above threshold, its logical error rate, each decoder's time per shot, and which decoder to use. On codes it never saw in training, its choice is good enough for 95% of circuits at 1.9× the time of the cheapest good decoder, where always running Tesseract costs 55×. With a GPU, it also plans for the GPU decoders.

TCBP-JOBS, GPU only

NVIDIA's new decoder, on a first slice of circuits.

NVIDIA · preliminary

The decoder of Howe, Maurya et al. (arXiv:2611.21321, preprint), through NVIDIA's CUDA-Q QEC nv-qldpc decoder: belief propagation on a model of the code fitted once per code, with JOBS post-processing. ECCentric runs it on every shot (the tcbp-jobs-sc preset), because BP can converge to the wrong logical class; the paper's default, which runs JOBS only where BP does not converge, was worse than Tesseract on 11 of 145 circuits.

On a slice of 306 circuits, all below threshold, each decoded with the same settings and shot budget as its CPU decoders:

  • never statistically worse than Tesseract or any CPU decoder (95% intervals);
  • as accurate as Tesseract on 281 circuits and more accurate on 5;
  • a median 306× less time per shot than Tesseract on one CPU core (9× to 29,000× for 80% of the circuits).

The settings of every decoder are documented in docs/decoders.md; the GPU decoders, how they were checked and how to run them in analysis/GPU.md.
