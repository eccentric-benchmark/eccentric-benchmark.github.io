How to read the results

The numbers

  • Logical error rate: the fraction of shots in which any logical qubit failed over all syndrome-extraction rounds (by default as many rounds as the code distance).
  • [95% CI]: the 95% Wilson confidence interval.
  • Per round: assumes independent rounds, ε = (1 − (1 − 2P)1/rounds)/2.
  • Shots: sampled until 100 logical errors, 100,000 shots or one hour of decoding, whichever comes first.

Status

measured
At least 100 logical errors seen.
low statistics
Stopped by the shot or time limit before 100 errors.
upper bound
No logical error seen; the 95% upper bound is given.
does not fit
The code needs more qubits than the device has.
decoder not applicable
E.g. matching on a code that is not matchable. Hover over it for the reason.
timeout after 9 h
The decoder did not finish a single batch of shots in time, typically a large code far above threshold.
⚠
The compiled circuit has distance 1: a single fault (e.g. on a SWAP between two data qubits that routing added) flips a logical qubit without any detector noticing.

Columns

  • Qubits: the code's data and ancilla qubits.
  • Circuit qubits: all qubits of the compiled circuit.
  • SWAPs: added by routing to fit the device.
  • 2q gates: two-qubit gates of the compiled circuit.

Codes, devices and decoders in the table link to their pages.

Devices

  • Marked *: projections, not announced devices: a chain of as many Loon or Flamingo QPUs as the code needs, each linked to the next by four l-couplers.
  • Native layout: a device built for the code, with a coupler for every pair of qubits its syndrome circuit entangles, so no SWAPs are needed. For bivariate bicycle codes this is IBM's planned layout, with its long-range couplers as good as nearest-neighbour ones.
  • Native, noisy long-range: the same layout with each qubit's two long-range couplers as noisy and slow as IBM's remote couplers (3% error, 300 ns; 0.3% at tenfold lower noise).
  • Decay while waiting: on devices with T1 and T2 (IBM, Infleqtion, Harvard/MIT), qubits decay for as long as they wait, for example while others are measured. IBM readout takes 1.56 µs, as on Heron today; the tenfold-lower IBM model has threefold longer T1 and T2.

Updates

  • Results with low statistics or that timed out are refined with more shots in the background, and updated here as they finish.
  • Until 28 October 2026, waiting qubits did not decay and IBM readout took 70 ns. The results on these devices have been recomputed since.
